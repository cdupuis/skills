# The tells

Every pattern from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" and its subpages (Style, Markup, Signs of AI use in comments), read 2026-11-22. Source: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing`. The page changes often; the raw wikitext is at the same URL with `?action=raw` if the list needs a refresh.

One or two of these in a text prove nothing. Several together are what a reader notices. Quoted phrases are what to look for, and none of them should appear in text written for cd.

Contents: content, words, sentence shapes, formatting, chatbot residue, messages and replies, change summaries, sources and links, Wikipedia-only signs, what does not count, what people do.

## Content

- Inflated significance: "stands as", "serves as a testament", "pivotal moment", "key turning point", "underscores its importance", "reflects broader", "setting the stage for", "evolving landscape", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted", "part of the wider". It turns up even for mundane facts, and after a concession ("though it saw limited use, it contributes to the broader history of").
- Saying a subject "has shaped discussions about" or "sparked debate about" other subjects.
- Describing coverage in place of content: "featured in", "profiled in", "documented across", "independent coverage", lists of outlets.
- "Sources identify X as", "his website lists him as", "records show that", where a plain statement would do.
- "Maintains an active social media presence", "a strong digital presence".
- Analysis bolted onto the end of a sentence as an -ing phrase: "highlighting", "underscoring", "emphasizing", "reflecting", "ensuring", "contributing to", "fostering", "showcasing". Also "valuable insights", "aligns with", "resonates with".
- Vague links: "associated with", "in connection with", "connected to". Often repeated through a text.
- Weasel attribution: "experts argue", "observers have noted", "industry reports", "some critics", "several sources" for one or two, "such as" in front of a complete list.
- The challenges formula: "Despite its [good thing], [subject] faces challenges", then "Despite these challenges", then an upbeat close or a "Future outlook".
- Headings of the form "X and Y", above all "Awards and recognition", "Challenges and future directions".
- Filling a gap with speculation: "likely", "maintains a low profile", "keeps personal details private".
- For living things: stressing ecosystem role and conservation status whether or not anything is known.

## Words

- The classic set (2023 to mid-2025): additionally, align with, boasts, bolstered, crucial, deep dive, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight (as a verb), interplay, intricate, intricacies, key, landscape (abstract), meticulous, pivotal, robust, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore (as a verb), valuable, vibrant.
- The 2026 set: challenge, clearer, dependable, echoed, foster, leverage, matters, multifaceted, practical, prioritize, quietly, steady, universally.
- Pseudo-scientific filler: causal, empirical, correlate.
- Promotional: nestled, in the heart of, breathtaking, rich (heritage, history, tapestry), profound, groundbreaking, renowned, diverse array, commitment to, natural beauty, exemplifies. Also the ones cd's first rule named: seamless, streamline, comprehensive, "key takeaways".
- Stiff synonyms: authored, relocated, utilized, attempted, passed away. People write wrote, moved, used, tried, died.
- These co-occur. Where one appears, look for the others.
- The list is literal. A word being overused does not make its synonyms suspect, so replacing "delve" with "dig" fixes nothing; the sentence needs a fact.

## Sentence shapes

- Dodging "is" and "has": "serves as", "stands as", "marks", "represents", "functions as", "operates as"; "features", "offers", "boasts" for "has"; "refers to" in a definition; "ventured into politics as a candidate" for "was a candidate".
- Negative parallelism: "not just X, but Y"; "not only X but also Y"; "it's not X, it's Y"; "no X, no Y, just Z"; "Y rather than X"; "rather than X, it does Y"; a pair of "praised X but questioned Y" items; the same move spread over two sentences with "however".
- Corrective framing (mid-2026 on): "should be treated as X rather than Y", "does not by itself establish", "should not be confused with", "should be distinguished from".
- Groups of three: three adjectives, three short phrases, three list items, often to make thin analysis look complete.
- Elegant variation: a new synonym each time the same thing is mentioned.
- A first sentence that defines the title as if it were a term: "X refers to", "The list of X is a curated compilation of".
- Em dashes for punch, usually with spaces around them, where a person would use a comma, a colon or parentheses.
- The overcorrection: sentences all between about 8 and 20 words, joined only by commas and full stops.

## Formatting

- Title Case In Headings.
- Bold scattered through prose, or every instance of a chosen phrase in bold, in a "key takeaways" manner.
- Vertical lists where each item opens with a bold label and a colon.
- Emoji in front of headings or list items.
- Small tables holding what a sentence or two could say.
- Curly quotation marks and apostrophes (a weak sign: word processors and phones produce them too).
- Heading errors: a top heading that repeats the title, skipped levels, several top-level headings, a heading with nothing under it but other headings.
- Markdown in a venue that shows it raw: asterisks for bold, `#` headings, `---` rules, `[text](url)` links, code fences around prose.
- Horizontal rules between sections.
- A long message cut into titled sections, or opened with a subject line.

## Chatbot residue

- Chat openers and closers: "Certainly!", "Of course!", "You're absolutely right!", "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Would you like", "let me know", "is there anything else", "here is a", "more detailed breakdown", "In this section, we will discuss".
- Advice to the requester left in the text: "ensure the content is neutral before publishing", "you can copy and paste this".
- Disclaimers: "it's important to note", "it's important to remember", "it is crucial to differentiate", "while specific details are limited", "not widely documented", "in the provided search results", "based on available information".
- "As an AI language model", "as of my last knowledge update", "I cannot do X, but I can".
- Summaries: "In summary", "In conclusion", "Overall", a section titled Conclusion, a paragraph that ends by restating itself.
- Placeholders: `[Your Name]`, `[link to the article]`, "(Add your URL here)", `2026-XX-XX`, `INSERT_SOURCE_URL`, `https://example.com/source1`, "Add if available".
- Notes for a reviewer inside the deliverable: "Delete this section before submission", "Reviewer note: this draft is neutral and well sourced".
- Text that stops in the middle of a sentence.
- Pasted chat turns ("You said:", "ChatGPT said:") and explaining the tool ("Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant").

## Messages and replies

- Letter form where nobody writes letters: "Dear", "I hope this message finds you well", "I am writing to", "Thank you for your time and consideration".
- Customer-service voice: "I have carefully reviewed", "I apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused", "If there are specific issues, please point them out and I will resolve them", "I would welcome any further feedback".
- Stock phrases: "I understand your concern regarding", "I understand the importance of", "I am happy to", "I respectfully request", "concrete evidence", "concrete examples", "I am committed to", "my aim is to", "moving forward, I will", "I assure you", "I have taken the time to".
- "A, particularly B": "the guidelines, particularly the ones on notability" where "the guidelines on notability" says it.
- Apologising for "any" harm that "may have" been caused. A person names the thing they did.
- Justifying each proposal by how much it improves things; reciting rules in bulk; citing a rule that does not exist or does not say that.
- Deflecting: "let's focus on the content rather than", "unsubstantiated speculation", asking what the "main concern" is when it was stated, asking for an "experienced, uninvolved" reviewer.
- "My intention is not to promote but to inform."
- Assuring the reader a human checked the text.
- Arguments laid out as bulleted lists or titled sections in what should be a short reply.

## Change summaries

Wikipedia calls these edit summaries. Commit messages, PR descriptions and changelog lines are the same genre.

- Praise in place of description: "improved clarity, flow, and readability", "refined tone", "enhanced", "streamlined", "enriched", "comprehensive rewrite".
- Assurances of compliance: "in line with the style guide", "ensures neutrality", "adheres to guidelines", stacked several to a summary.
- Saying what was not changed: "while preserving the original meaning", "retained", "avoided", "ensured", "aimed to". This echoes the prompt that asked for the change.
- Stressing that content is "sourced", "verified", "with citations" and saying nothing of what it says.
- Itemising every parameter, template or bit of markup touched.
- "Addressed reviewer feedback" with nothing specific.
- First person, markdown, emoji, or a chatbot preamble in the summary.
- What a person writes: short, specific, often abbreviated. "removed excess links per MOS:OVERLINK".

## Sources and links

- Several dead links, with no archived copy.
- DOIs that do not resolve, ISBNs with a bad checksum, DOIs that resolve to an unrelated paper.
- Book citations with no page number, or with a page that does not support the claim.
- References defined and never used; the same reference reused with the wrong syntax; the ↩ character.
- Tracking parameters: `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, `utm_source=openai`, `utm_source=copilot.com`, `referrer=grok.com`.
- Leaked internal markup: `contentReference[oaicite:1]`, `oai_citation`, `turn0search0`, `[cite: 1]`, `grok_card`, `attached_file`, `ppl-ai-file-upload`.
- Access dates a year older than the text.

## Wikipedia-only signs

Listed for completeness; they don't arise outside a wiki. Markdown inside wikitext, nonexistent categories and templates, an article wrapped in nowiki tags, a draft created with a "declined" banner already on it, other pre-placed maintenance tags, "submission statements" for reviewers, user pages with "About Me" and "Let's Connect!" sections, made-up policy shortcuts or a bare "WP", maintenance banners transcluded by accident, a sudden change in an editor's style, many small edits made quickly to gain permissions.

## What does not count

The page calls these ineffective indicators: perfect grammar, formal or academic prose, bland prose, a mix of casual and formal registers, a single transition word, missing citations, odd markup that a broken editor produces. Don't treat any of them as a tell and don't "fix" them.

## What people do

The page lists habits found more often in human text. Keep them.

- Simple constructions: "there is a", "it has a".
- Plain words over formal ones.
- Superlatives and flat claims: "one of the best", "is the only", "was the first".
- Qualifiers and intensifiers: "very", "perhaps", "tends to".
- The occasional wordy phrase: "as a result of", "in order to", "all of the", "the fact that".
- Being able to say why they wrote what they wrote.
